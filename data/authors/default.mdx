I grew up in Kolkata and enjoyed solving math problems in middle school.
My time at ISI Bangalore gave me a structured way of thinking about abstract mathematical ideas.
I am interested in problems that require a formal quantitative framework to reason about meaningfully. That is what led me to statistics, and eventually to computational biology.

My masters in Statistics at ETH Zürich focused on statistical inference and nonparametric methods.
I have always had a parallel interest in biology, and computational biology felt like a natural way to bring that together with mathematics and statistics.
At the [Robinson Statistical Bioinformatics Group](https://robinsonlabuzh.github.io), working on omics data for the first time solidified that interest.

I am currently a PhD student in Systems Biology at the Department of Molecular Life Sciences, University of Zürich, shared between the labs of [Silvia Domcke](https://domcke.github.io/domcke-lab.html) and [Mark Robinson](https://robinsonlabuzh.github.io).
My work focuses on questions in single-cell perturbation studies.
I also have a broader interest in the benchmarking of computational methods.
I enjoy the informatics challenges that come with the field and care about building FOSS tools that are efficient and widely usable.

I am very passionate about open science and reproducible research, and I strive to ensure that my work upholds these standards.
You can find more about the specifics of my academic work on my [cv page](/cv).

I also enjoy listening to music.
